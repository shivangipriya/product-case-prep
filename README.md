# Product Case Prep

Paste a product interview question or case prompt. Get a structured outline to think in: clarifying questions to ask first, then the sections a strong answer usually moves through, with hints for the type of question it looks like. The structure guides; the answers are yours.

**Try it:** https://shivangipriya.github.io/product-case-prep/ - runs in your browser, no signup.

## Why this exists

Product interviews reward clear structure under time pressure. The structure is learnable; the thinking is not copyable. This tool holds the structure so prep time goes into the thinking.

## What it does

- **Question-type detection.** Design, improvement, metrics and strategy questions each get their own outline; anything else gets a general one. Detection is keyword-based and the label is shown, so a wrong label is easy to spot and the general outline still works.
- **Clarifying questions first.** Each type seeds the questions worth asking the interviewer before answering, with space for your own.
- **Sectioned outline.** Users and problems, options with tradeoffs, a justified pick, metrics and guardrails, risks: the sections a strong answer usually moves through, each with hints and room for your own words.
- **Copyable outline.** Your notes under each heading copy out as a clean prep document. No scoring, no verdict on whether the answer is good.

## Privacy

Everything runs in the page on your device. Nothing you type is sent anywhere, saved, or kept after you leave.

## What this tool does not do

- It does not answer the question. It holds a structure while you think.
- Question-type detection is keyword-based; if the label looks wrong, the general outline still works.
- No structure guarantees a good interview. Interviewers reward clear thinking, not filled templates.
- It never scores your outline and never says whether an answer is right.

## Testing

Detection is tested against design, improvement, metrics and strategy phrasings, including overlapping cases (a "how would you measure" question about a redesign must classify as metrics), a general fallback, and the completeness of every outline (each type ships clarifying questions and at least four sections with hints).
